The short version

Most businesses I work with are better than they look online.

I spent more than twenty years in hospitality and owned three cafés. I know what a quiet Wednesday looks like in the bank account on Thursday, and how much of a week can hang on whether someone found your menu on their phone.

I see the same thing over and over. A business has built a good name, the referrals are coming in and the regulars are loyal. Then a new customer Googles them and finds an old website, photos taken on a phone between services and a list of services that stopped being accurate a couple of years ago. The business has moved on and the marketing hasn’t. That gap costs enquiries, and most owners never find out how many.

The Service Edit started with email. My first project was setting up Meat Merchant’s email marketing in Klaviyo. Websites came next, then photos, because I kept building good sites and filling them with photos that undersold the business. These days I often do all three for the same client, because they work better when one person is looking after them.

It’s just me. There’s no account manager and no junior doing the work. You’ll deal with me from the first email through to launch, and I’m the one sending your campaigns after that.

What I do

Websites, photos and email, working together.

A lot of businesses end up with a web designer, a photographer and someone doing their emails, and none of them have ever spoken to each other. I’d rather look after all three myself. The photos suit the site, the emails send people somewhere that works, and you only have to explain your business once.

01

Websites

Someone looking at your website is usually trying to work out what you do, whether you’re any good and how to book, order or send you the job. I make those answers easy to find on a phone, and set the site up so Google understands what you do and where you do it.

02

Photos

Most businesses already have the photos. They’re on a phone, taken in bad light between customers. I upgrade them so the dish, the job or the room looks the way it does in person, without adding anything that isn’t there. You can use them on your site, your Google profile, your emails and your listings, with or without a new website.

03

Email marketing

Every booking, order and QR code on a table gives you a customer’s email address, and most businesses never send those people anything. On a monthly retainer I set up the platform properly, write and send campaigns in your voice, and build the emails that go out on their own, like a welcome, a birthday message or a nudge to someone who hasn’t been in for a while. I work in Klaviyo and Mailchimp.

What running cafés taught me

A few things I work by.

01

People look you up in a hurry.

Nobody browses a café website at a desk. They’re on the footpath or in the car, deciding where to go in the next twenty minutes, and a tradie’s customer is often doing the same thing between jobs. So I build for the phone first and cut anything that slows them down.

02

Referrals still check you out.

Plenty of good businesses run on word of mouth. But the person who got your name from a friend will still Google you before they get in touch, and if what they find looks half finished, some of them won’t bother. A lot of my work is making sure that quick check goes your way.

03

Sometimes the cheaper option is right.

If all you need is a simple site that says who you are and how to reach you, I’ll tell you. I’d rather you spend the money where it makes a difference, even if that means spending less with me.

Straight answers

Before you get in touch.

Who runs The Service Edit?

I do. I’m Mel Cox, and The Service Edit is a one person studio in Perth, Western Australia. You’ll deal with me from the first email through to launch, and after that too. There’s no account manager and nobody junior doing the work.

What do you actually do?

I look after the websites, photos and email marketing for service businesses. That might mean building a new website, upgrading the photos you already have so things look the way they do in person, or running your email marketing each month: setting up Klaviyo or Mailchimp, writing and sending campaigns, and building the emails that go out on their own. Some clients have me do all of it, others just one part. I don’t run social media accounts, but the photos I upgrade are yours to post wherever you like.

What kinds of businesses do you work with?

Mostly hospitality and trades, plus e-commerce, venues and a few that don’t fit a category. So far that includes a laneway café in Northbridge, a pizza and margarita bar in Casuarina, a commercial plasterer on the Gold Coast, a global venue finding business, a hand-binding book studio in Wollongong, a handmade fishing lure maker and an online meat retailer. I also upgrade listing photos for real estate agents.

Do you only work with Perth businesses?

No. I’m based in Perth and work with businesses right across Australia. One of them, CVBS, finds venues around the world.

Why hire a café owner to do your marketing?

Because I’ve been on the other side of it. I’ve paid for marketing out of my own takings and wondered whether it did anything. Running cafés taught me where the money actually comes from: the table booked on a phone at 5pm, the regular who comes back because an email reminded them, the new customer who picked you because your photos looked like the real thing. That’s what I build for. Jordy’s did 22 per cent more covers this winter than last.

How do I start?

Send me your website and a line about your business. I’ll go through it and tell you plainly what I think is costing you enquiries. If a new website isn’t the answer, I’ll tell you that too, and what I’d do instead.
