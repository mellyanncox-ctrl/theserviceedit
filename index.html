One build, taken apart

The five layers under a website that works.

CVBS has been sourcing conference venues for Australian organisations since 1989. Their buyers are executive assistants and event organisers who can't afford to get the venue wrong. Here is what went into the new site before anyone saw a colour.

01 · Strategy

The expensive part starts before the design.

Who needs to find you, what they need to know, and what happens next. An EA booking a conference isn't shopping for a venue. They're looking for someone they can trust with the decision. So the site leads with the people, the process and a shortlist in 48 hours, not a search box.

02 · Copy

Good copy doesn't sound like everyone else.

"Find your perfect venue" is on every booking site. "The right venue isn't searched. It's sourced." says what CVBS does that a search box can't. Every generic line is cut until the site sounds like the business, not the internet.

03 · Search

The best website is useless if nobody finds it.

Every destination, from Sydney to the Hunter Valley, has its own page, connected to the services behind it, so Google understands what CVBS does and where. Search isn't something added afterwards. It is the architecture.

04 · Enquiries

Traffic isn't the end goal. Enquiries are.

Every section has a job. The hero makes the promise, the process removes the risk, the team and client stories give proof, and every page leads to one next step: submit a brief. Nothing sits on the page without a reason.

05 · Build

Designing it is only half the job.

It has to work on a phone between meetings and hold up on a desktop in the office. A brief form that keeps your progress, pages that load fast, layouts that hold at every screen size. The part nobody sees is the part that decides whether it works.
