Why email works so well for hospitality

Venues have advantages most industries would love: customers who already like them, and something worth saying every week that isn’t a discount.

Mel CoxPerth4 min read

Email works for most businesses, but hospitality has a few advantages that most industries would love to have. If you run a café, restaurant or bar, they’re worth understanding, because they change how much your list is actually worth.

People already know you

A software company emailing a free trial user is starting from almost nothing. A venue emailing someone who had dinner there last month isn’t. That person has sat at your tables, eaten your food and, hopefully, had a good night. When your name turns up in their inbox, it isn’t a stranger selling something. It’s a place they already like.

That’s a big part of why a well-kept hospitality list gets opened. At Jordy’s, segmented sends open at up to 68 per cent, because each email goes to the guests it’s relevant to rather than the whole list at once.

You have a reason to write every week

Most businesses struggle to find something worth saying. Venues don’t. There’s a new special, a menu change, a public holiday, a band, a long weekend. Most weeks something is happening that your regulars would want to know about, and none of it has to be a discount.

People decide often

Someone buying a car decides once every few years. Someone deciding where to have dinner decides several times a week. So every week there’s a new chance to be the place they pick, and an email that lands at the right time can tip it. Early in the week for a midweek table, Thursday for the weekend.

The list builds itself

Most venues are collecting contacts without really thinking about it: online bookings, online orders, QR codes, event sign-ups. Each one adds someone who has already chosen you. The list grows every week you’re open, and you don’t pay for a single new contact.

Why most independents aren’t doing it

The big groups have marketing teams and set this up years ago. Independents mostly haven’t, and it isn’t because they don’t see the value. Running a venue leaves very little time for anything that isn’t urgent, and email sounds technical: platforms, deliverability, segments, automations. So the list sits there.

Once it’s set up properly, a lot of it runs on its own. A welcome email goes to every new guest. A birthday email goes out on the day. A win back email goes to people who haven’t been in for a while. The weekly campaign is the only part that needs writing each time, and that’s the part I do for clients.

Worth checking this week

If you take bookings through NowBookIt or orders online, have a look at how many email addresses you’ve collected. Then ask when those people last heard from you. If the answer is never, that’s the easiest place to start.
